Privacy Policy

Effective: October 2026 · for App: Privnum

Summary

Privnum works entirely offline on your device. No account, no analytics, no crash reporting, no ads, no servers. The app does not even request network access.

Data Stored on Your Device

Privnum stores the following data locally in app-private storage:

  • Contacts — names, phone numbers, emails, notes and other fields you enter, held in an encrypted database
  • Contact photos — pictures you attach, stored as individually encrypted files next to the database
  • Settings — theme, popup toggles, default region, passcode data (salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hash, encrypted preferences)

The contact database and every contact photo are encrypted on the device with keys held in the platform keystore. System backup of app data is disabled, and data-extraction rules exclude everything from both cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. Uninstalling the app deletes everything.

Backup and Restore

You can export contacts as a VCF file to a folder you choose (including cloud folders mounted on your device) and re-import it later. The file is written by Android's document picker to your chosen location; nothing is uploaded by the app itself.

Permissions

  • Phone state — detect incoming/outgoing calls to show the caller popup
  • Call log — read your recent calls when you open the in-app call history, and resolve call state where the system requires it. Requested only from an explicit button you tap, never automatically; the list is read on demand and is never stored in the app database or sent anywhere
  • Display over other apps — show the caller popup during calls (optional, with in-app rationale)
  • Notifications — call-related notifications on Android 13+
  • Disable keyguard — let the caller popup appear over the lock screen during a call
  • Biometrics — use fingerprint or face unlock for the app's passcode screen, if you enable it

The app does not request camera, microphone, location, or network access. Photos come from the system gallery picker or capture intent.

The app does not read your address book. It registers a caller-lookup provider guarded by the READ_CONTACTS permission, which lets the system resolve a caller for the dialer using numbers you have entered in Privnum — the app never reads the platform contact list itself. Biometric hardware is used only for the local unlock check: nothing biometric is stored or exported, and a biometric match releases a token kept on the device under a keystore-bound key.

Third Parties

No third-party SDKs with data collection are embedded. Tapping a WhatsApp/Telegram row opens those apps (or their websites) with the number you chose to contact — that interaction is governed by their policies. See THIRD_PARTY.md in the repository for open-source licenses.

No Analytics or Tracking

Privnum embeds no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting, or telemetry SDKs. Nothing about how you use the app ever leaves your device.

Security

  • Contact database and contact photos encrypted at rest; keys generated on the device and held only in the platform keystore, never exportable
  • Passcode verified with a salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 derivation, compared in constant time, with a 5-attempt lockout; biometric unlock kept AES-GCM-wrapped under an authentication-bound keystore key
  • Screenshots and screen recording blocked across the whole app
  • All data in app-private storage; cloud backup and device-to-device transfer both disabled
  • Release builds strip debug logs

Encryption has no recovery path. The key exists only in the platform keystore and there is no cloud copy to restore from, so a lost key means unrecoverable contacts — you would have to import a VCF backup. Events that destroy the key (uninstall, factory reset, wiping the device) also erase the app data holding the contacts, so the practical exposure is limited to keystore corruption.

Your Rights

You have full control over your data:

  • Access — everything is visible inside the app
  • Export — save a VCF backup at any time
  • Deletion — delete contacts individually or all at once, remove the passcode, or uninstall the app to erase all data

There is no account to close and no server copy to request.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and no service in it collects personal information from children.
